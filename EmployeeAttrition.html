Analytics Dashboard
Employee Attrition

An end-to-end HR analytics workflow that cleans and transforms employee data using Power Query, then analyzes and visualizes employee attrition patterns through Power BI, highlighting workforce demographics, job roles, and key factors associated with employee turnover.


This report presents an analysis of employee attrition using a fictional dataset developed by IBM data scientists. The dataset is based on employee-related information and is intended for analytical and educational purposes rather than representing the actual workforce or operations of a specific organization. The original dataset is publicly available through Kaggle and can be accessed here.

The primary objective of this report is to provide a clear overview of employee attrition across different departments and to examine the employee characteristics associated with attrition. By analyzing attrition at the departmental level, the report aims to highlight areas where employee turnover may be more prevalent and provide a more detailed understanding of the workforce profiles within those areas.

The analysis considers several employee attributes, including age, marital status, years of service, job role, and monthly rate classification. These characteristics are visualized alongside employee attrition information to identify patterns and relationships within the dataset. For example, examining attrition across different job roles or years of service can provide insight into which employee groups experience higher levels of turnover within the fictional organization.

The dashboard is designed to allow users to explore these factors interactively and compare attrition patterns across departments and employee profiles. Rather than looking at attrition as a single overall percentage, the report breaks the data down into several dimensions to provide a more comprehensive view of the workforce. This approach demonstrates how data visualization and exploratory analysis can be used to identify potential areas of concern and support evidence-based discussions around employee retention.

For the data preparation process, Power Query was used to clean, transform, and manipulate the source dataset. This included preparing the data for analysis and ensuring that the relevant fields were structured appropriately for reporting. Power BI was then used to develop the data model, create visualizations, and build the interactive dashboard presented in this report.

Overall, this project demonstrates the use of data cleaning, transformation, analysis, and visualization techniques to turn raw employee data into an interactive analytical report. It provides an example of how HR analytics can be used to examine employee attrition patterns and explore the factors that may be associated with workforce turnover.
